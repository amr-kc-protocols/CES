What this sample misses most

Across every in-scope chart loaded. The element at the top of this list is the one worth a shift briefing — it is the question a reviewer is most often left asking.

By report author

Ordered by how many charts each person wrote, not by score. This is a list of who to teach and what to teach them — it is not a ranking, and a low mean on three charts means very little. Grouped by the report's author rather than the whole crew: counting a chart against everyone on the truck tells a partner they write badly when they may not have written it at all. Where the chart names no author, the first crew member is assumed and marked as such.

No charts loaded

Add IFT ePCR exports above to begin. The tool reads each chart against the seven medical-necessity elements from the IFT Field Handbook and reports which ones the documentation establishes.

It does not decide whether a transport was medically necessary — it cannot see the patient, and the problem it exists to solve is that the chart does not say. It reports whether a claim reviewer could reach that conclusion from the record, and which question stopped them.

Incident

All processing is local. This application has no network code path: no fetch, no XHR, no analytics, no sync. Charts and notes are stored in this browser's IndexedDB on this device only. Use “Clear all data” when finished on a shared machine.

This tool grades the record, not the transport and not the crew. It applies deterministic keyword and field checks with citations, intended to direct attention and guide coaching. It is not a billing determination, not a compliance audit, and not a clinical judgement.
