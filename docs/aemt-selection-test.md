# AEMT Program Selection Test

**AMR Kansas City — earn-while-you-learn AEMT cohort**
Draft 1 · requires Program Manager and Medical Director approval before use

---

## What this instrument is for

It contributes **40%** of a candidate's composite selection score. It is one input
among four, plus an additional-duty bonus, and it is not a rank-ordering device
on its own. The composite model lives in `aemt-selection-interview.md`.

It measures four things the course will demand on day one:

| Section | Measures | Items | Time |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | EMT-level knowledge retention | 20 | 25 min |
| B | Dosage arithmetic | 10 | 20 min |
| C | Reading comprehension of unfamiliar clinical text | 10 | 20 min |
| D | Situational judgment — competing demands | 8 | 15 min |
| | | **48** | **80 min** |

### What it deliberately does not measure

**No AEMT content.** Candidates have not been taught it. Testing it measures
prior exposure — who happened to work alongside a medic who explained IV
therapy — not aptitude, and it predicts completion poorly while advantaging
candidates for reasons unrelated to the job.

**No drug knowledge in Section B.** Every clinical value needed is supplied in
the question stem. A candidate who has never given a medication can score full
marks if they can do the arithmetic. That is the point: the course teaches the
pharmacology, it does not teach the maths.

---

## Administration

- Proctored, closed-book, no phones. A basic calculator is **permitted** for
  Section B and prohibited elsewhere.
- All candidates sit the same version on the same day where possible.
- Read the section time limits aloud; do not extend them. Working under time
  pressure is part of what is being observed.
- Candidates with an approved accommodation receive it. Route those requests
  through HR before test day and do not record the nature of the accommodation
  on the answer sheet.
- Scoring is done by a scorer who did not interview the candidate, against the
  key below, before interview scores are discussed.

## Section A — EMT knowledge retention

*20 items · 25 minutes · no calculator · 1 mark each*

1. An oropharyngeal airway is correctly sized by measuring from the corner of
   the mouth to which landmark?
   a) The cricoid cartilage b) The angle of the jaw
   c) The sternal notch d) The bridge of the nose

2. A nasopharyngeal airway is contraindicated in a patient with:
   a) An intact gag reflex b) Suspected basilar skull fracture
   c) Snoring respirations d) A dental appliance

3. A non-rebreather mask should be run at what oxygen flow rate?
   a) 2–4 L/min b) 6–8 L/min c) 10–15 L/min d) 20–25 L/min

4. A nasal cannula is appropriate at what flow range?
   a) 1–6 L/min b) 8–10 L/min c) 12–15 L/min d) 15–25 L/min

5. The correct adult chest compression rate is:
   a) 60–80/min b) 80–100/min c) 100–120/min d) 120–140/min

6. Adult chest compression depth should be:
   a) At least 1 inch b) At least 1.5 inches
   c) At least 2 inches d) At least 3 inches

7. Single-rescuer adult CPR uses what compression-to-ventilation ratio?
   a) 15:2 b) 30:2 c) 5:1 d) 15:1

8. Immediately after an AED delivers a shock, the rescuer should:
   a) Check a pulse for 10 seconds b) Resume chest compressions
   c) Reanalyse the rhythm d) Deliver two rescue breaths

9. For a conscious adult with suspected acute coronary syndrome and no
   contraindication, the usual aspirin dose is:
   a) 81 mg b) 160–325 mg c) 500 mg d) 650 mg

10. Nitroglycerin should be withheld if the patient:
    a) Has taken a PDE-5 inhibitor (e.g. sildenafil) within 48 hours
    b) Has a history of angina c) Is over 65 d) Has already taken aspirin

11. The Cincinnati Prehospital Stroke Scale assesses:
    a) Pupils, grip strength, gait
    b) Facial droop, arm drift, speech
    c) Level of consciousness, blood glucose, blood pressure
    d) Facial droop, leg drift, vision

12. Oral glucose is contraindicated when the patient:
    a) Has type 1 diabetes b) Cannot protect their own airway
    c) Has a blood glucose below 60 mg/dL d) Has already eaten

13. An epinephrine auto-injector is administered into the:
    a) Deltoid b) Gluteal muscle
    c) Lateral thigh d) Abdominal subcutaneous tissue

14. Naloxone is given to reverse which effect?
    a) Benzodiazepine sedation b) Opioid-induced respiratory depression
    c) Alcohol intoxication d) Stimulant-induced tachycardia

15. When applying a commercial tourniquet to a bleeding extremity, you should:
    a) Place it directly over the wound
    b) Place it proximal to the wound and tighten until bleeding stops
    c) Place it distal to the wound and release every 10 minutes
    d) Place it over a joint for better compression

16. Which set of findings best describes **compensated** shock?
    a) Hypotension, bradycardia, warm dry skin
    b) Tachycardia, narrowing pulse pressure, pale cool skin, anxiety
    c) Hypotension, altered mental status, absent radial pulse
    d) Normal vitals with reported dizziness only

17. The normal adult respiratory rate at rest is:
    a) 6–10/min b) 12–20/min c) 20–28/min d) 28–36/min

18. The Pediatric Assessment Triangle consists of:
    a) Airway, breathing, circulation
    b) Appearance, work of breathing, circulation to skin
    c) Alertness, breath sounds, capillary refill
    d) Age, weight, level of consciousness

19. Under START triage, an adult with spontaneous respirations above 30 per
    minute is tagged:
    a) Minor (green) b) Delayed (yellow)
    c) Immediate (red) d) Expectant (black)

20. In the SAMPLE history, the "E" stands for:
    a) Exposure b) Environment
    c) Events leading to the present illness or injury d) Evaluation

## Section B — Dosage arithmetic

*10 items · 20 minutes · calculator permitted · 2 marks each*

**Read first:** every value you need is given in the question. You are not
expected to know any medication. Show your working — partial credit is
available where the method is right and the arithmetic slips.

1. A vial contains 50 mg in 5 mL. How many mL contain 30 mg?

2. A patient weighs 176 lb. What is their weight in kilograms?
   (1 kg = 2.2 lb)

3. A medication is ordered at 0.1 mg per kilogram. The patient weighs 80 kg.
   What is the total dose in mg?

4. You need 8 mg of a drug supplied at 10 mg/mL. What volume do you draw up?

5. 500 mL is to be infused over 4 hours using a set that delivers 10 drops per
   mL. What is the rate in drops per minute, to the nearest whole drop?

6. 1,000 mL is to run over 8 hours. What is the hourly rate in mL?

7. A 50 mL syringe contains 25 g of dextrose. What percentage solution is this?

8. An order is written for half of a 0.4 mg dose. How many mg is that?

9. An infusion is started at 1420 and runs for 90 minutes. At what time does it
   finish? (24-hour clock)

10. A 1:1,000 concentration contains 1 mg per mL. What volume delivers 0.3 mg?

## Section C — Reading comprehension

*10 items · 20 minutes · no calculator · 1.5 marks each*

> ### ⚠ This passage is a test instrument, not a clinical protocol.
> It is fictional, written for this examination, and must never be used to
> guide patient care. Candidates should be told this before they read it.

### Passage — "Regional Sepsis Alert Criteria" (sample text)

> A Sepsis Alert may be initiated by any credentialed provider when a patient
> meets the entry condition **and** at least two qualifying findings.
>
> The entry condition is a suspected or confirmed source of infection. A source
> reported by the patient, a family member, or a receiving facility is
> sufficient; laboratory confirmation is not required in the prehospital
> setting.
>
> Qualifying findings are: temperature above 38.0 °C or below 36.0 °C;
> respiratory rate above 22 per minute; heart rate above 90 per minute; systolic
> blood pressure below 100 mmHg; and altered mental status from the patient's
> baseline.
>
> A patient meeting the entry condition and two or more qualifying findings is
> designated **Sepsis Alert — Tier 1**. A patient who additionally has a
> systolic blood pressure below 90 mmHg after a fluid challenge, or a lactate
> reported above 4.0 mmol/L, is designated **Tier 2**.
>
> Tier 2 requires direct physician notification before arrival. Tier 1 requires
> notification to the charge nurse only.
>
> Patients under 18 years of age are excluded from this pathway and are managed
> under the paediatric febrile illness guideline. Patients receiving palliative
> or hospice care are excluded unless the patient or their representative
> requests full intervention.
>
> Where a patient meets Tier 2 criteria but transport time is under 8 minutes,
> the alert is downgraded to Tier 1 and physician notification is made on
> arrival rather than en route.

### Questions

1. A 40-year-old with a reported urinary infection has a heart rate of 104 and a
   respiratory rate of 24. Does this patient meet Sepsis Alert criteria, and at
   which tier?

2. A 55-year-old has a temperature of 38.4 °C and a heart rate of 96, but no
   suspected infection source has been identified by anyone. Do they meet
   criteria? Explain in one sentence.

3. A patient meets Tier 2 criteria. Transport time is 6 minutes. What tier is
   declared, and who is notified, and when?

4. Does a lactate value need to be obtained before declaring Tier 1?

5. A 16-year-old meets every listed qualifying finding and has a confirmed
   infection. What does the passage direct you to do?

6. A hospice patient's daughter, acting as their representative, requests full
   intervention. The patient meets the entry condition and three qualifying
   findings. Are they eligible for the pathway?

7. List the two ways a patient can be designated Tier 2.

8. A patient has a systolic blood pressure of 96 mmHg and an altered mental
   status, with a suspected pneumonia. What tier, and why?

9. Who may initiate a Sepsis Alert under this passage?

10. A patient meets the entry condition and exactly one qualifying finding.
    What, if anything, is declared?

## Section D — Situational judgment

*8 items · 15 minutes · 2 marks each*

For each scenario, choose the **single best** response. There is no trick — some
options are simply better than others.

1. **Six weeks in.** You are two chapters behind and the next exam is in nine
   days. You:
   a) Read everything the night before the exam
   b) Map the remaining chapters across the nine days and book the time in your
      calendar, starting tonight ✔ 3
   c) Ask a classmate for their notes and skip the reading ✔ 1
   d) Ask the instructor to move the exam ✔ 0

2. **Your shift ran two hours over** and class starts in 40 minutes. You:
   a) Skip class and catch up from the recording ✔ 1
   b) Attend late, tell the instructor why, and get the material you missed ✔ 3
   c) Skip class and say nothing ✔ 0
   d) Attend but leave early to make up sleep ✔ 1

3. **You fail a quiz** at 68%. You:
   a) Review which topics you missed and rework them before the next class ✔ 3
   b) Wait to see whether the next quiz goes better ✔ 1
   c) Ask whether the quiz can be dropped ✔ 0
   d) Study everything again from the start ✔ 2

4. **A clinical shift is offered** on your only free day this week, and you are
   behind on reading. You:
   a) Take the shift; the reading can wait ✔ 1
   b) Decline and read ✔ 2
   c) Take the shift, and move two hours of reading into the following two
      evenings before accepting ✔ 3
   d) Ask someone else to take it and say nothing about why ✔ 1

5. **Your study group** meets weekly but has become mostly social. You:
   a) Stop attending ✔ 1
   b) Propose a specific agenda for the next meeting and bring it ✔ 3
   c) Keep attending and study alone as well ✔ 2
   d) Complain to the instructor ✔ 0

6. **You do not understand IV drip rate calculations** after the lecture. You:
   a) Hope it is not on the exam ✔ 0
   b) Ask the instructor before leaving, or email that evening ✔ 3
   c) Search online and try to work it out alone ✔ 2
   d) Ask a classmate the day before the exam ✔ 1

7. **Family obligation lands** on a class night, four weeks out. You:
   a) Tell the instructor now and arrange to make up the content ✔ 3
   b) Tell the instructor that day ✔ 1
   c) Miss the class and catch up later ✔ 1
   d) Bring the obligation with you ✔ 0

8. **You are managing well** and a classmate is visibly struggling. You:
   a) Focus on your own work ✔ 1
   b) Offer to work through the difficult material together once a week ✔ 3
   c) Tell the instructor they are struggling ✔ 2
   d) Give them your completed work to look at ✔ 0

### Section B — 2 marks each (20 total)

| # | Answer | Method |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **3 mL** | 50 mg ÷ 5 mL = 10 mg/mL; 30 ÷ 10 |
| 2 | **80 kg** | 176 ÷ 2.2 |
| 3 | **8 mg** | 0.1 × 80 |
| 4 | **0.8 mL** | 8 ÷ 10 |
| 5 | **21 gtt/min** | 500 ÷ 240 min = 2.083 mL/min × 10 = 20.83 |
| 6 | **125 mL/h** | 1000 ÷ 8 |
| 7 | **50%** | 25 g ÷ 50 mL = 0.5 g/mL = 50 g/100 mL |
| 8 | **0.2 mg** | 0.4 ÷ 2 |
| 9 | **1550** | 1420 + 90 min |
| 10 | **0.3 mL** | 1 mg/mL, so 0.3 mg = 0.3 mL |

Award 1 of 2 marks where the method is correct and the arithmetic is wrong.

### Section C — 1.5 marks each (15 total)

1. **Yes — Tier 1.** Entry condition met (reported UTI) plus two qualifying
   findings (HR 104, RR 24).
2. **No.** The entry condition requires a suspected or confirmed infection
   source; vital-sign findings alone do not qualify.
3. **Tier 1**, charge nurse notified, on arrival. Transport under 8 minutes
   downgrades it.
4. **No.** Lactate appears only in the Tier 2 criteria.
5. **Manage under the paediatric febrile illness guideline** — under-18s are
   excluded from this pathway.
6. **Yes.** The hospice exclusion is lifted when the patient or their
   representative requests full intervention.
7. **(i)** systolic BP below 90 mmHg after a fluid challenge; **(ii)** lactate
   above 4.0 mmol/L.
8. **Tier 1.** Entry condition plus two qualifying findings (SBP <100, altered
   mental status). SBP 96 is not below 90, and no lactate is given.
9. **Any credentialed provider.**
10. **Nothing.** Two or more qualifying findings are required.

### Section D — 2 marks each (16 total)

Score the chosen option by the value marked ✔ beside it, then scale: an option
worth 3 = 2 marks, 2 = 1.3, 1 = 0.7, 0 = 0.

## Scoring and thresholds

**Total available: 71 marks.** Convert to a percentage for the composite score.

### Recommended thresholds

| Rule | Threshold | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| Overall pass | **70%** | Below this, the candidate is starting behind on material the course assumes |
| **Section B floor** | **70% (14/20)** | Hard floor. Mid-course maths remediation is the single hardest thing to deliver to a working student, and this section is pure arithmetic with every value supplied — a low score here is a genuine risk signal, not a knowledge gap |
| Section C floor | 60% | Predicts ability to self-study from the textbook |

A candidate who clears the overall bar but fails the Section B floor should not
be advanced on the strength of interview performance. That is precisely the
trade that produces a non-completion.

**Leave the seat empty.** With 4–5 seats and completion as the priority, a
candidate below threshold costs more than an unfilled chair.

## Before you use it

1. **Pilot it.** Give it to 6–10 current AEMTs and paramedics who have already
   passed NREMT, plus a few EMTs who have not attempted it. If the thresholds do
   not separate those groups, move the thresholds — do not move the candidates.
2. **Medical Director review** of Sections A and B for scope and accuracy.
3. **HR review** of Section D and the administration instructions.
4. Have someone who did not write the items sit it cold and time each section.
   The limits above are estimates.

## Records

Retain completed papers, scoring sheets and the composite score for every
candidate — selected and not — with the accommodation requests filed separately.
This is a selection procedure under the EEOC Uniform Guidelines, and the
records are what demonstrate it was applied consistently.

Note that these are **not** the K.A.R. 109-17-3 program records and are not
covered by that three-year retention clock. Keep them under your own HR
retention schedule.
