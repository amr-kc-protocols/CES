**AMR Kansas City — earn-while-you-learn AEMT cohort**
Draft 1 · requires HR review before use

Companion to `aemt-selection-test.md`.

---

## Composite scoring model

Retention is handled by the service commitment agreement signed at acceptance,
not by selection. Selection therefore measures one thing: **who is most likely
to complete the course and pass the certification examination.**

### Base — 100 points

| Component | Weight | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Selection test | **40** | `aemt-selection-test.md` |
| Structured interview | **30** | This document |
| QA chart review scores | **20** | Trailing 12 months, CES QA module |
| Attendance record | **10** | Trailing 12 months, HR record |

### Bonus — up to 5 points

Demonstrated willingness to take on additional duty, which is behavioural
evidence rather than a stated intention.

| | Bonus |
|---|---|
| Current EMT Field Training Officer in good standing | **+5** |
| Preceptor, CE instructor, peer mentor, or FTO within the last 24 months | **+3** |
| None | 0 |

**What this bonus does and does not do.** It decides a tie and a near-tie. It
cannot carry a candidate over a threshold from a weak score — 64 + 5 is 69 and
still fails. That is deliberate: FTO service predicts effort, not arithmetic.

**One thing to check before you finalise it.** Weighting FTO status inherits
whatever process selected FTOs. If that was consistent and documented, this is
strong evidence. If it was informal, you are importing it into a formal
selection procedure — worth a look with HR.

### Thresholds

| Rule | Threshold |
|---|---|
| Composite pass | **70** |
| Selection test overall | 70% |
| Selection test Section B (dosage) | 70% — hard floor |
| Selection test Section C (comprehension) | 60% |
| Interview | 18 / 30 |

**Leave the seat empty.** With four to five seats and completion as the stated
priority, a candidate below threshold costs more than an unfilled chair.

## Interview format

- **Two interviewers.** One should be the Program Manager or primary instructor;
  the other should not be the candidate's direct supervisor.
- **Same questions, same order, every candidate.** No follow-ups beyond the
  listed probes. Improvised questions are where consistency dies.
- **Score independently before you confer.** Write your scores down, then
  compare. Where two scorers differ by 2 or more points on the same question,
  discuss what each of you heard and re-score — do not average.
- **Take notes on what was said**, not on impressions. "Said he blocks Sunday
  mornings for study" is a record. "Seemed motivated" is not.
- 45 minutes. Six questions.

### Opening script — read it as written

> Thanks for coming in. This is a structured interview, so I'll ask the same six
> questions I ask everyone, in the same order, and we'll both be taking notes.
> There are no trick questions. Most of them ask for a specific example from
> your own experience — take a moment to think of one before you answer, that's
> normal and it's fine.
>
> Before we start, I want to be straight about what the program actually is,
> because I'd rather you turn it down now than discover it in week six.

Then give the realistic job preview: class nights and times, the sixteen-week
span, the clinical and field shift commitment on top of your regular schedule,
the attendance policy, and the service agreement. **Say the numbers out loud.**
A candidate who withdraws at this point has saved you a seat.

## Questions and anchored scoring

Each question is scored **1 to 5**. Anchors are given for 1, 3 and 5; use 2 and
4 for answers that fall between.

---

### Q1 — Self-directed learning

> **"Tell me about something you've learned or gotten better at in the last
> year that nobody required you to. Walk me through how you actually went about
> it."**

*Predicts: capacity to study without being chased. The single largest demand of
this program.*

**Probes:** How did you find the time? What did you use? How did you know you
were making progress?

| | |
|---|---|
| **1** | Cannot produce an example, or names something entirely passive with no structure behind it |
| **3** | A genuine example with some structure, but time was found ad hoc — whenever it happened to be free |
| **5** | A specific example, a deliberate plan, protected time they can describe, and some way they knew it was working |

---

### Q2 — Competing demands

> **"Describe a stretch when work, home, and something else you'd committed to
> all landed at once. What did you actually do?"**

*Predicts: the exact failure mode of a working student. This is the highest-value
question in the set.*

**Probes:** What did you drop? Who did you tell, and when? What would you do
differently?

| | |
|---|---|
| **1** | Describes being overwhelmed with no strategy, or claims this has never happened to them |
| **3** | Coped, but reactively — managed it by working harder rather than by deciding anything |
| **5** | Describes triage, explicit trade-offs, and telling the affected people early rather than after the fact |

---

### Q3 — Response to failure

> **"Tell me about a time you failed at something that mattered to you. What
> happened next?"**

*Predicts: response to a failed exam or a failed skill check-off — which most
students will have at least once.*

**Probes:** What did you change? How long before you acted?

| | |
|---|---|
| **1** | Blames circumstances or other people, or offers no example |
| **3** | Owns it and recovered, but what actually changed is vague |
| **5** | Owns it specifically, names the change they made, and can say how they knew it worked |

---

### Q4 — The commitment, against their next six months

> **"You've heard what the schedule is. Looking at that against your next six
> months, where do you see the pressure points, and what's your plan for them?"**

*Predicts: realism. A candidate who says "no problem" is a worse bet than one who
names a genuine constraint and a plan for it.*

> ### ⚠ Interviewer guard rail
> Ask about **the plan**. Never probe the reason. If a candidate volunteers a
> personal circumstance, acknowledge it and return to the plan — do not record
> it, do not ask follow-ups about it, and do not let it affect the score. You
> are scoring the quality of the planning, not the shape of anyone's life.

**Probes:** What have you already arranged? What would you do if that fell
through?

| | |
|---|---|
| **1** | Dismisses the difficulty entirely, or cannot identify a single pressure point |
| **3** | Identifies real pressure points, but the plan is general — "I'll make it work" |
| **5** | Identifies specific pressure points and specific arrangements they have already thought through or made |

---

### Q5 — Receiving correction

> **"Tell me about a time someone corrected you on a call or on a report. How
> did you take it, and what happened after?"**

*Predicts: how they will handle preceptor feedback and a failed skill sheet
criterion.*

**Probes:** What did you say at the time? What did you do differently
afterwards?

| | |
|---|---|
| **1** | Defensive framing, or presents the correction as unfair |
| **3** | Accepted it; whether anything changed afterwards is unclear |
| **5** | Sought clarification, changed their practice, and can name the change |

---

### Q6 — Why AEMT, why now

> **"Why AEMT, and why now?"**

*Predicts: durability of motivation through week eleven.*

**Probes:** What made now the right time? What do you want to be doing in three
years?

| | |
|---|---|
| **1** | Pay alone, or an answer that could apply to any program |
| **3** | A clear personal reason |
| **5** | A clear reason tied to how they want to practise, and to staying in the role |

---

## What must not be asked

Not by either interviewer, not as small talk, not as a follow-up:

- Family arrangements, childcare, dependants
- Pregnancy, or plans to have children
- Age, or when they graduated
- Health, disability, or fitness
- Religion, national origin, ethnicity, citizenship beyond work authorisation
- Marital or relationship status
- Arrest record
- Second jobs — this reads as availability but functions as an economic-status
  proxy

If a candidate volunteers any of the above, do not record it and do not follow
it up. Anything used to select employees for a funded program is a selection
procedure under the EEOC Uniform Guidelines, and the notes are discoverable.

## Before you use it

1. **Calibrate the interviewers.** Both of you score the same practice
   interview — a colleague playing a candidate — and compare before you see a
   real one. If you are more than a point apart on most questions, work through
   the anchors together until you are not.
2. **HR review** of the question set, the guard rail on Q4, and the prohibited
   list.
3. Decide in advance what you will do if only three candidates clear 70. The
   answer should be "run three," and it is much easier to hold that line before
   you know who they are.

## Records

Retain the scoring sheets, the notes and the composite for **every** candidate,
selected or not, under your HR retention schedule. These are not K.A.R. 109-17-3
program records and do not sit under that three-year clock.
